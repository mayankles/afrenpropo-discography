The Mars Volta: Studio Albums Discography

Musicians & Instrumentation (2003–2025)

The Mars Volta is the volatile, shape-shifting partnership of vocalist/lyricist Cedric Bixler-Zavala and guitarist/composer/producer Omar Rodríguez-López—childhood friends from El Paso, Texas, who torched their acclaimed post-hardcore band At the Drive-In at its commercial peak in 2001 to chase something stranger. What they built was the most audacious progressive rock project of the 2000s: a fusion of prog ambition, punk violence, salsa, dub, free jazz, and Latin psychedelia, delivered in surreal Spanglish and governed by Omar's near-dictatorial creative control. Everyone beyond the duo is, in effect, a hired collaborator—which explains the band's constant, dramatic lineup turnover.

Death and the occult shadow their story. Founding sound manipulator Jeremy Michael Ward died weeks before their debut's release in 2003; his found-diary became the basis of Frances the Mute, and his loss haunts the early catalog. The classic era (2003–2006) featured drummer Jon Theodore's polyrhythmic power and deep Red Hot Chili Peppers involvement—Flea played bass on most of De-Loused in the Comatorium, and John Frusciante appeared on every album of the era. Gospel prodigy Thomas Pridgen powered the maximalist, Ouija-cursed Bedlam in Goliath (a Grammy winner) and its restrained inversion Octahedron, before avant-garde drummer Deantoni Parks drove the compressed "future punk" of Noctourniquet. Exhaustion and resentment split the band in January 2013; beloved keyboardist Ikey Owens, core to every album through 2009, died the following year.

After a decade of side projects, reconciliation, and shared crisis—including the Scientology harassment campaign waged against Cedric's family—the duo returned in 2022 with a shock reinvention: concise, Caribbean-rooted psychedelic pop, followed by a full acoustic "folk" re-recording and 2025's jazz-forward Lucro Sucio; Los Ojos del Vacío, with Wayne Shorter sideman Leo Genovese as the new era's key voice. Twenty-plus years in, they remain what they've always been: a band that treats genre as disposable and reinvention as survival.

Scope: the nine studio albums credited to The Mars Volta. Excludes the Tremulant EP, live albums (Scabdates), the leaked Ramrod Tapes demos, the La Realidad de Los Sueños box set, and side projects (At the Drive-In, De Facto, Antemasque, Bosnian Rainbows, Omar's 50+ solo albums). Que Dios Te Maldiga Mi Corazón (2023) is included as the band's official eighth studio album despite being an acoustic re-recording of the 2022 record.

⭐ Noctourniquet
2012		 Cedric, Omar, Alderete, Marcel(keys/synths) +Deantoni Parksdrums (replacing Pridgen) Omar (producer) Warner. Synth-heavy "future punk"—tighter songs, electronic textures, mythology drawn from Solomon Grundy and the Hyacinth myth. #15 US. Recorded amid tension (Cedric cut vocals largely apart from the band). The group dissolved acrimoniously in Jan 2013; Cedric & Omar reconciled in Antemasque (2014) before the long silence. Keyboardist Ikey Owens died in 2014 while touring with Jack White.
