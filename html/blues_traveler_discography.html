Blues Traveler: Studio Albums Discography

Musicians & Instrumentation (1990–2023)

John Popper and Brendan Hill started playing together in high school in Princeton, New Jersey, in the mid-1980s, in a garage band that went through names such as the Establishment and Blues Band before settling on Blues Traveler, a nod to Gozer the Traveler from Ghostbusters. Guitarist Chan Kinchla and bassist Bobby Sheehan completed the lineup that moved to New York, played the club circuit, and signed with A&M Records. Popper is the band's centre of gravity: a singer and songwriter whose harmonica playing, fast enough to be a calling card and a running joke, treats the instrument as a lead guitar. Around him, Chan Kinchla supplies guitar and mandolin, Hill the drums and percussion, and Sheehan was the melodic, elastic bass underneath. The self-titled debut (1990) captured a jam-structured band built on blues riffs, and Travelers and Thieves (1991) and Save His Soul (1993) deepened it while the live following grew. In 1992 the band helped found the H.O.R.D.E. touring festival, a template for the jam-band circuit of the decade.

Four (1994) turned that cult following into a mainstream hit. Recorded at Bearsville Studios in Woodstock, New York, with producers Michael Barbiero and Steve Thompson, it peaked at No. 8 on the Billboard 200, went six times platinum in the U.S., and produced "Run-Around" (No. 8 on the Hot 100, and the 1995 Grammy winner for Best Rock Performance by a Duo or Group) and "Hook." The same team returned for Straight On till Morning (1997), which reached No. 11 and went platinum. It would be the last album with Sheehan. He died of an accidental drug overdose in New Orleans on August 20, 1999, and the band had to decide whether to continue. They did, adding Tad Kinchla, Chan's younger brother, on bass after an audition, and keyboardist Ben Wilson in January 2000, which gave the group a keyboard voice it had never had before.

The post-Sheehan years were rebuilding years. A free online release in 2000, Decisions of the Sky, came first, then Bridge (2001), the band's last album for A&M, which dropped them in 2002. Since then Blues Traveler has worked across a string of labels, among them Sanctuary, Vanguard, Verve Forecast, 429, Loud & Proud, BMG and Round Hill, and has largely stayed independent of the major-label chart game while remaining a reliable touring act, a regular July 4 headliner at Red Rocks since 1994 (except 1999 and 2020). The albums of this era range widely: a producer-driven roots record with the late Jay Bennett (¡Bastardos!, 2005), a record built entirely on guest duets with younger pop and rock acts (Blow Up the Moon, 2015), and a pair of covers albums, one of blues standards and one of soul.

Traveler's Blues (2021), produced by Matt Rollings with guests including Keb' Mo', Warren Haynes, Kingfish Ingram and The War and Treaty, reached No. 1 on Billboard's Top Blues Albums chart and was nominated for the Grammy for Best Traditional Blues Album. Traveler's Soul (2023) followed with R&B and soul covers. In 2024 the band toured Four in full for its thirtieth anniversary, and in 2025 it co-headlined with Gin Blossoms and Spin Doctors, releasing a cover of R. Dean Taylor's "Indiana Wants Me" with them, with further 2026 dates announced. All five members of the current lineup (Popper, Chan Kinchla, Hill, Tad Kinchla and Wilson) are still together, though no new studio album since Traveler's Soul turned up in research for this report.

Scope: covers Blues Traveler's studio albums, 1990–2023, sixteen in all, plus Decisions of the Sky (2000), which was released free online as a short digital set rather than a conventional album and is tagged as such. Excluded: live albums (Live from the Fall, Live: What You and I Have Been Through, Live on the Rocks, On Tour Forever, ¡Bastardos en Vivo!), compilations (Travelogue, 25, Icon, The Definitive Collection), and members' solo work, including John Popper's solo albums and side projects, since Blues Traveler itself has been the constant. Contested or unverified details are flagged in the rows: several producer credits and the label of Cover Yourself conflict between sources or could not be confirmed. Blow Up the Moon, Traveler's Blues and Traveler's Soul are tagged by format because they are guest-driven or cover records. Years follow the discography listing, so Traveler's Blues is dated 2021 although its release was reported as delayed from July to October.
