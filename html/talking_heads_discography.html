Talking Heads: Studio Albums Discography

Musicians & Instrumentation (1977–1988)

David Byrne and Chris Frantz started playing together as the Artistics at the Rhode Island School of Design in 1973, then moved to New York and recruited Frantz's girlfriend Tina Weymouth on bass — she learned the instrument specifically for the job, auditioning three times before Byrne would take her. As Talking Heads, the trio played their first show on June 5, 1975, opening for the Ramones at CBGB, and signed to Sire Records in November 1976. Jerry Harrison, formerly of the Modern Lovers, joined on keyboards and guitar in January 1977, completing the lineup that would stay fixed for the rest of the band's existence. Their debut, Talking Heads: 77, produced by Tony Bongiovi and Lance Quinn, translated the nervous, cerebral energy of the Bowery scene into something tighter and stranger than punk — clipped guitar figures, deadpan vocals, and "Psycho Killer" as its calling card.

The band's defining transformation came from three consecutive albums produced by Brian Eno. More Songs About Buildings and Food (1978), cut at Compass Point in Nassau, loosened the debut's tension into funkier, more textured arrangements and gave the band a hit with its cover of Al Green's "Take Me to the River." Fear of Music (1979) pushed further into paranoid, polyrhythmic territory, with King Crimson's Robert Fripp guesting on guitar. Remain in Light (1980) went further still, building layered, single-chord grooves modeled on Fela Kuti and King Sunny Adé with an expanded cast including Adrian Belew, Jon Hassell, and Nona Hendryx. It's now regarded as the band's masterpiece, but making it fractured the group: advance pressings credited the songwriting to "Byrne, Eno, Talking Heads" rather than all four members by name, Frantz and Weymouth felt sidelined during the New York sessions, and the ensuing dispute ended the band's working relationship with Eno.

A three-year hiatus followed, during which Weymouth and Frantz launched the hit side project Tom Tom Club and Byrne and Eno released My Life in the Bush of Ghosts. Talking Heads regrouped to self-produce (with Alex Sadkin) 1983's Speaking in Tongues, whose "Burning Down the House" became their only U.S. top-ten single and whose limited Robert Rauschenberg-designed sleeve won a Grammy. Jonathan Demme's concert film Stop Making Sense (1984) captured the band at its live peak with an expanded touring ensemble, though it isn't a studio release. Little Creatures (1985) and True Stories (1986, tied to Byrne's own film of the same name) pivoted toward American roots and pop songcraft and became the band's commercial high-water mark, before Naked (1988), cut in Paris with Steve Lillywhite and guests including Johnny Marr, returned to the worldbeat sound of Remain in Light.

Talking Heads dissolved in December 1991; Frantz has said he learned of Byrne's departure from a Los Angeles Times article rather than from Byrne directly. The remaining three toured and recorded as The Heads in the mid-1990s, a name Byrne opposed as trading on the band's legacy, and the personal rift has lingered in public since — Weymouth has called Byrne "incapable of returning friendship." A brief reunion took place at their 2002 Rock and Roll Hall of Fame induction, and the four appeared together again in 2023 around the IMAX reissue of Stop Making Sense, though Byrne has repeatedly ruled out a reunion tour or new music; the band reportedly turned down an $80 million tour offer in early 2024. In June 2025, marking the band's 50th anniversary, they released a new "Psycho Killer" music video directed by Mike Mills and starring Saoirse Ronan.

Scope: covers all eight Talking Heads studio albums, 1977–1988. Excluded: live albums (The Name of This Band Is Talking Heads, Stop Making Sense), compilations and remix releases, the 2018 expanded True Stories soundtrack combining the film cast's versions (distinct from the band's own True Stories album, which is included), and post-breakup work released under the name The Heads (1996), which Byrne never took part in. Also out of scope, per the default for a single-band catalog: members' side and solo projects (Tom Tom Club, Jerry Harrison's and David Byrne's solo work, My Life in the Bush of Ghosts), since Talking Heads itself was the constant rather than a rotating cast of side bands.

1983		 David Byrnevocals, guitar, keyboards Chris Frantzdrums, synthesizer Tina Weymouthsynth bass, double bass Jerry Harrisonkeyboards, guitar Alex Sadkin, co-producer, with the band; Wally Badarou & Bernie Worrell (synths), Alex Weir (guitar), Steve Scales & David Van Tieghem (percussion), Nona Hendryx & Dolette McDonald (vocals) Sire.
