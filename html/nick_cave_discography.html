Nick Cave: Complete Discography

Studio Albums Across All Bands & Projects (1979–2024)

The Boys Next Door

Cave's first band, formed in Melbourne in 1977 from school friends. Began as a glam/punk-inflected outfit before mutating into post-punk. The launchpad for nearly every long-term Cave collaborator.

Glam-tinged post-punk owing a debt to Bowie. By the time of release the band was already shifting toward the harsher sound that would define their next incarnation.

The Birthday Party

The Boys Next Door renamed after relocating to London (1980). One of post-punk's most violent, confrontational bands — blues, free jazz, and rockabilly weaponized behind Cave's snarling delivery. Hugely influential on gothic and noise rock.

Nick Cave and the Bad Seeds

Cave's main vehicle since 1983 and one of post-punk's most enduring acts — 18 studio albums spanning gothic blues, murder ballads, tender piano confessionals, and late-career ambient grief. Founded with Mick Harvey and Blixa Bargeld; Warren Ellis became the central creative foil from the 2000s onward.

1997		 Cave, Harvey, Bargeld, Wydler, Casey, Savage, Ellis Spare, confessional piano ballads. Widely regarded as a masterpiece. "Into My Arms."
No More Shall We Part
2001		 Cave, Harvey, Bargeld, Wydler, Casey, Savage, Ellis; prod. Tony Cohen Return after a four-year gap and recovery from addiction. Stately, hymnal, melancholy.
Nocturama

Abattoir Blues / The Lyre of Orpheus
2004		 Cave, Harvey, Casey, Savage, Ellis, Sclavunos, Wydler; + gospel choir Ambitious double album recorded after Bargeld's exit. Blues, gospel, and rock; a critical resurgence.
Dig, Lazarus, Dig!!!
2008		 Cave, Harvey, Casey, Ellis, Sclavunos, Wydler Looser, groove-driven sound directly influenced by the Grinderman side project. Mick Harvey's final album before leaving in 2009.
Push the Sky Away
2013		 Cave, Warren Ellis (now central), Casey, Sclavunos, Wydler, George Vjestica First of the late "ambient trilogy." Warren Ellis's loops and textures move to the foreground. "Jubilee Street."
Skeleton Tree
2016		 Cave, Ellis, Casey, Sclavunos, Wydler Recorded around the death of Cave's son Arthur in 2015. Devastating, fractured, ambient. Accompanied by the film One More Time with Feeling.
Ghosteen
2019		 Cave, Ellis, Casey, Sclavunos, Wydler Double album closing the trilogy; an almost beatless meditation on grief and loss. Widely hailed as one of his greatest works.
Wild God
2024		 Cave, Warren Ellis (prod.); mixed by Dave Fridmann Eighteenth studio album. A turn back toward warmth, communal uplift, and qualified joy after years of grief.

Note: This lists the band's 18 studio albums and omits live albums (e.g. Live Seeds, Idiot Prayer) and compilations.

Grinderman

A raw, garage-rock side project (2006–2011) drawn from a Bad Seeds subset, built to recapture the primal aggression of Cave's early bands. Its loose, riff-driven approach directly fed back into the Bad Seeds' Dig, Lazarus, Dig!!!

Nick Cave & Warren Ellis

The duo billing for Cave's longtime collaborator and Bad Seeds musical director. Beyond their dozen-plus film scores (The Proposition, The Assassination of Jesse James, The Road), they released a proper studio album in the wake of the pandemic.

Overview of Nick Cave's Musical Career:

One continuous thread: Across nearly five decades, Nick Cave's bands form a remarkably continuous lineage. The Boys Next Door became The Birthday Party, which dissolved into Nick Cave and the Bad Seeds, which spawned Grinderman and the Nick Cave & Warren Ellis duo. Key collaborators recur throughout: Mick Harvey was with Cave from the very beginning (1977) until 2009, and Warren Ellis—who first appeared on the Bad Seeds' mid-90s records—gradually became Cave's primary creative partner and the band's musical director.
The Boys Next Door & The Birthday Party (1977–1983): Cave's formative bands, rooted in Melbourne's punk scene and later London's post-punk underground. The Birthday Party in particular—with guitarist Rowland S. Howard and bassist Tracy Pew—became legendary for their violent intensity and remain hugely influential on gothic, noise, and post-punk music.
The Bad Seeds (1983–present): Cave's defining work. The band's 18 studio albums chart an extraordinary evolution: from the gothic blues chaos of the mid-80s, through the narrative-driven peak of Let Love In and Murder Ballads, the tender piano confessionals of The Boatman's Call, and finally the ambient, grief-stricken late trilogy (Push the Sky Away, Skeleton Tree, Ghosteen) before the cautious uplift of Wild God. Founding members Mick Harvey and Blixa Bargeld were central to the early sound; Warren Ellis defines the modern era.
Grinderman (2006–2011): A garage-rock side project formed by Cave with Warren Ellis, Martyn P. Casey, and Jim Sclavunos—all Bad Seeds—to reconnect with the raw aggression of his early bands. Its two albums fed energy and looseness back into the parent band.
Nick Cave & Warren Ellis: Best known for their acclaimed film scores, the duo also released Carnage (2021), a studio album born of the pandemic that extended the sparse, atmospheric sound of the Bad Seeds' recent work. Ellis is also Cave's partner on numerous soundtracks and the Seven Psalms (2022) project.
Recurring personnel: Mick Harvey (multi-instrumentalist, 1977–2009), Blixa Bargeld (guitar, 1983–2003), Warren Ellis (violin/loops/keys, mid-90s–present), Martyn P. Casey (bass), Thomas Wydler (drums), Jim Sclavunos (drums/percussion), Conway Savage (keys, d. 2018), and Barry Adamson (early bass). Cave has also released collaborations with Anita Lane and others, and recorded scores and the spoken/ambient Seven Psalms; this report focuses on his core studio band albums.
Scope note: This covers Cave's studio albums across his bands and the Cave/Ellis duo. It excludes the many film soundtracks he and Ellis have composed, live albums, compilations, and B-sides/rarities collections, any of which I can break out into a separate table if useful.
