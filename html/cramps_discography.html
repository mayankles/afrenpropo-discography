The Cramps: Studio Albums Discography

Musicians & Instrumentation (1980–2026)

Lux Interior and Poison Ivy Rorschach met in Sacramento in 1972, bonded over art and record collecting, and moved to Akron, Ohio in 1973 and then to New York in 1975, where they landed in the CBGB scene. The first full lineup, in April 1976, paired the couple with guitarist Bryan Gregory and drummer Pam Balam; Miriam Linna replaced Balam, and Nick Knox took the drum stool in September 1977. Nobody else in the New York scene sounded like them: no bass, a wall of reverb-soaked guitar, and a singer who treated 1950s rockabilly, surf, garage and horror-movie kitsch as scripture. They cut their first singles in Memphis in 1977 with Alex Chilton producing at Ardent Studios, and Miles Copeland signed them to I.R.S. Records.

Lux and Ivy are the constant. Lux was the frontman, lyricist and visual designer, and Ivy was the guitarist, arranger and, on most records, co-producer, the person who decided what a song needed and what it didn't. Everyone else rotated around them. Chilton returned for the 1980 debut Songs the Lord Taught Us, tracked at Phillips Recording in Memphis. Soon afterward the band relocated to Los Angeles, where Gregory was replaced by Kid Congo Powers of the Gun Club. That lineup made Psychedelic Jungle (1981), the band's first self-produced album, and then went quiet. A dispute with Copeland over royalties and creative rights led to a court case that held up new releases until 1983. The live album Smell of Female and the singles compilation …Off the Bone arrived that year as stopgaps, and Powers left around the same time.

The band's second life began with 1986's A Date with Elvis, on the independent Big Beat label, with Ivy covering bass herself before Candy Del Mar became the permanent bassist. They stayed on that footing through Stay Sick! (1990), the last studio album with Knox, whose departure with Del Mar in 1991 opened the era of Slim Chance on bass and a revolving drum chair: Jim Sclavunos on Look Mom No Head!, then Harry Drumdini, who drummed from 1993 to 2003 and again from 2006 to 2009. Flamejob (1994) and Big Beat from Badsville (1997) were recorded at Earle Mankey's home studio in Thousand Oaks. The band made some of its most visible pop-culture appearances in these years, including a spot on Late Night with Conan O'Brien in 1994 and a 1995 Halloween episode of Beverly Hills, 90210. Their only UK top-40 single, "Bikini Girls with Machine Guns," came from Stay Sick!.

Fiends of Dope Island (2003), the first release on their revived Vengeance Records, was the last studio album. The band played its final show on November 4, 2006, at the Marquee Theater in Tempe, Arizona. Bryan Gregory had died on January 10, 2001, at age 49, and Lux Interior died on February 4, 2009, of an aortic dissection, which ended the band. Poison Ivy has since looked after the catalog. In 2026 she revived Vengeance with In The Red's Larry Hardy and engineer Jimmy Maslon under the banner The Cramps Inc., and issued Gravest Gravy, a set of shelved October 1977 Chilton sessions. The Cramps never charted high, and their influence is easier to hear in psychobilly, garage punk and goth than in their sales.

Scope: all studio albums from Songs the Lord Taught Us (1980) to Fiends of Dope Island (2003), plus three releases that are not studio albums but were chosen by the club or sit in the catalog's gaps, each tagged in the table: the live album Smell of Female (1983), the compilation …Off the Bone (1983), and the archival Gravest Gravy (2026), a collection of 1977 sessions. Excluded: the EPs Gravest Hits (1979), Blues Fix (1992) and Ultra Twist (1994); the other compilations (Bad Music for Bad People, Greatest Hits, How to Make a Monster); the live album RockinnReelininAucklandNewZealandXXX (1987); and the filmed Live at Napa State Mental Hospital (1978). These are omitted to keep the report focused on the core catalog. …Off the Bone is a compilation of singles and EP tracks, and some of its material is shared with other releases. Songs the Lord Taught Us was released on Illegal in the UK and I.R.S. in the US, and sources differ on which counts as the original label. No side projects are covered, since the band was the constant and the members' other work was minor.

1980		 Lux InteriorvocalsPoison Ivy RorschachguitarBryan GregoryguitarNick Knoxdrums Alex Chilton, producer, and credited as "Booker C" on organ on "Fever"; John Hampton, engineer Illegal (UK) / I.R.S. (US). Recorded 1979–1980 at Phillips Recording in Memphis. A bass-less, reverb-heavy debut built on rockabilly, garage and surf covers, including the Sonics' "Strychnine" and a "Fever" taken from Little Willie John. "Garbageman" was the only single. Later critics rated it highly, and Rolling Stone put it on a 2020 list of the best albums of 1980.

1981		 Lux InteriorvocalsPoison Ivy RorschachguitarKid Congo PowersguitarNick Knoxdrums Self-produced by the Cramps; Paul McKenna, engineer I.R.S. (US/UK). Recorded January 1981 at A&M Studios in Hollywood, released March 27. Seven of the 14 tracks are Interior/Rorschach originals, with the rest drawn from 1950s and 60s rock and roll, rockabilly and garage singles, including "Green Door" and "Goo Goo Muck." The latter found new audiences through The Texas Chainsaw Massacre Part 2 (1986) and the TV series Wednesday. Its release was followed by the royalty dispute with Miles Copeland that stalled new releases until 1983.
